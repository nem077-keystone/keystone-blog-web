When it comes to choosing a cryptocurrency wallet, the options can be overwhelming, especially for beginners. With so many different types of wallets offering various features, it can be tricky to know which one is best suited for your needs. But don’t worry! We’re here to break it down in a simple way.

At its core, every crypto wallet does the same thing: it stores your private keys and gives you an interface to manage your crypto assets. However, the key difference lies in where these wallets store your private keys and how secure they are.

So, how do you choose the right wallet for your needs? Before choosing a wallet, ask yourself these questions:

1. Does it offer self-custody?
2. Is it Secure?
3. Does it support Multiple Networks?
4. Does It Support Different Asset Types?
5. What Type of Wallet is Best for Security?
6. What About Multisig Wallets?

Let’s dive into the key factors you should consider before picking a wallet.
# 1. Does It Offer Self-Custody?

When choosing a wallet, it's important to know who controls your crypto. **Self-custody wallets**, like [Keystone](https://keyst.one/), let you take full control of your private keys, which means **you** own and manage your crypto directly. You’re in charge of its security, and no one else can access it. On the other hand, **custodial wallets** are managed by third parties, like exchanges. In these wallets, they hold your private keys, which means they technically own your crypto and you’re trusting them with your assets. If you value security and control, self-custody wallets are the best choice because **you** remain in control.

# 2. Is It Secure?

Security is a top priority when it comes to storing your cryptocurrency. The level of security largely depends on where and how the wallet stores your private keys. For instance, software wallets store private keys on devices connected to the internet, which can be vulnerable to hacks. Hardware wallets take a different approach by keeping your private keys offline, significantly reducing the risk of remote hacks.

# 3. Does It Support Multiple Networks?

Not all wallets work with multiple blockchain networks. Some wallets are limited to just one network, meaning you’ll need separate wallets for different types of assets, like Bitcoin and Ethereum. 

# 4. Does It Support Different Asset Types?

Within the same blockchain network, wallets can differ in terms of the assets they support. Some wallets may only handle coins, while others can manage tokens and NFTs as well. Keystone supports a wide range of digital assets, from Bitcoin to NFTs, ensuring flexibility in managing your crypto portfolio.

# 5. What Type of Wallet is Best for Security?

## Hardware vs. Software Wallets

Software wallets are popular for their convenience and ease of use. However, they come with risks, as your private keys are stored on devices connected to the internet, making them more susceptible to hacks. Hardware wallets, on the other hand, provide a much safer option by storing private keys offline. [Keystone 3 pro](https://keyst.one/shop/products/keystone-3-pro), for example, combines the security of a hardware wallet with user-friendly features like a large touchscreen and compatibility with popular software wallets like MetaMask.

# 6. What About Multisig Wallets?

For those who want extra security or need to share control of an account, multisignature (multisig) wallets can be a good solution. Multisig wallets require multiple signatures to approve a transaction, which can protect your assets if one key is compromised. 

# Conclusion

Choosing the right crypto wallet depends on your specific needs—whether it’s security, asset type support, or ease of use. For those who prioritize security and control over their assets, hardware wallets like Keystone are the best option. Keystone offers a range of advanced features, from secure elements to seamless QR code transactions, making it the perfect companion for both beginners and seasoned crypto users.
