# Getting to Know Bitcoin Storage Models

Before deciding how to store your seed phrase, it’s important to understand the type of storage model you’re using. Each setup has its own risks and benefits, which will influence the best way to back up your data. Keystone, with its advanced security solutions like multi-signature support and Shamir’s Secret Sharing, is a versatile tool for all these models.

## Here are the four primary Bitcoin storage methods:

**Single-Signature (Singlesig):** A straightforward setup where one wallet corresponds to one seed phrase. While simple, it’s risky since anyone who gets hold of your seed phrase has access to your Bitcoin.
**Single-Signature with a Passphrase**: Adds an extra layer of protection by combining a custom passphrase with your seed phrase. The catch? Lose the passphrase, and you lose your Bitcoin.
**Multi-Signature (Multisig)**: Involves multiple keys to access your funds, offering better protection against theft or accidental loss. Keystone excels in this space by providing user-friendly multisig functionality with seamless integration.
**Shamir’s Secret Sharing Scheme (SSSS):** Breaks your seed phrase into several pieces, only requiring a specific number of them to restore access. Keystone hardware wallets support SLIP-39 for effortless implementation.

# How Storage Models Impact Backup Strategies

**1. Singlesig**
	•	Why It’s Popular: It’s simple—one wallet, one seed phrase.
	•	The Downside: If your seed phrase is compromised, so are your funds.
	•	Pro Tip: Use [Keystone 3 Pro](https://keyst.one/shop/products/keystone-3-pro)’s metal backup compatibility to safeguard against fire and water damage. Store it securely, and ensure only you know its location.

**2. Singlesig + Passphrase**
	•	Enhanced Security: Combines a 12- or 24-word seed phrase with a passphrase for an added level of protection.
	•	Know the Difference: Unlike a wallet PIN, a passphrase is an integral part of your wallet’s setup.
	•	Storage Advice: Store your seed phrase and passphrase in separate, safe locations. Keystone’s intuitive interface simplifies managing passphrase-enabled wallets.

**3. Multisig**
	•	How It Works: Requires multiple keys (e.g., 2 out of 3) to authorize transactions.
	•	Why It’s Secure: Losing one key doesn’t mean losing your funds, and attackers also need details about your wallet configuration.
	•	Best Practices: Keystone’s QR code and SD card functionality make synchronizing multisig wallets simple. Spread your keys across multiple secure locations, like a home safe or a bank safety deposit box.

**4. Shamir’s Secret Sharing Scheme (SSSS)**
	•	The Concept: Divides your seed phrase into parts, like 3 out of 5, so you only need a subset of them to restore your wallet.
	•	Advantages: Flexible and resilient—losing a part doesn’t mean losing access.
	•	Tips for Storage: Spread the parts across different safe spots. Keystone’s support for SLIP-39 ensures smooth setup and usability.

## The Best and Worst Places to Store Seed Phrases

**Avoid These:**
	•	Open spaces
	•	Desk drawers or cupboards
	•	Filing cabinets
	•	Workplaces
	•	Bags or backpacks

**Why? These locations are easily accessible to others, raising the risk of theft or accidental loss.**

Choose These Instead:
	1.	**Home Safe**: Keystone’s compatibility with metal backups ensures your seed phrases are protected from environmental hazards.
	2.	**Secondary Home Location**: Consider hiding a backup in a separate area of your home, like under floorboards or in an outdoor shed.
	3.	**Safety Deposit Box**: Ideal for multisig or SSSS setups, Keystone’s wallet structure ensures data remains safe and decentralized.
	4.	**Trusted Family Member**: For multisig or SSSS setups, a reliable family member can safeguard one of your backups, reducing risk without centralizing access.

**Things to Keep in Mind for All Storage Models**
	1.	Use Metal Backups: Keystone 3 Pro offers metal backup compatibility for ultimate protection.
	2.	Secure Access: Ensure only you (or a highly trusted individual) know where your backups are stored.
	3.	Avoid Overcomplication: Keystone’s user-friendly design helps prevent errors or loss due to complexity.
