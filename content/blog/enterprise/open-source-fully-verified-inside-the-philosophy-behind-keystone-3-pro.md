Keystone doesn’t just talk about transparency — we live it. With [Keystone 3 Pro](https://keyst.one/shop/products/keystone-3-pro?variant=KV032), every line of code and every piece of hardware is open to the public, proving we have nothing to hide and everything to protect.

# Why Open Source Is the Future of Crypto Security, and How Keystone 3 Pro Leads the Way

In crypto, **“not your keys, not your coins”** is a mantra. But here’s one more to remember:
**“Don’t trust, verify.”**

At [Keystone](https://keyst.one/), open source isn't just a buzzword. It's a belief system. One that guides every decision we make, from the code that powers your wallet to the hardware that keeps it safe.

With the launch of Keystone 3 Pro, we’re doubling down on open source, transparency, and security. Here’s how — and why it matters to you.

# Open Source ≠ Just “Public Code”

Let’s bust a myth: Open source doesn’t mean just uploading code to GitHub and calling it a day.

At Keystone, we treat open source as a design principle, not a checkbox. It shapes how we build, test, and secure everything — from firmware to hardware. And yes, we open source it all:

- ✅ Firmware
- ✅ Hardware blueprints
- ✅ Secure element architecture
- ✅ Audit reports
- ✅ Signing logic

This level of transparency is unmatched in the hardware wallet industry, and it’s how we hold ourselves accountable to the crypto community.

# Real Engineering, Real Challenges — Solved the Open Way

**Creating secure systems while keeping the code public isn’t easy.**

For example, in closed systems, developers can hide encryption keys within the software — but open source makes that vulnerable. Instead of hiding, we innovated. Every Keystone device generates its own unique encryption keys at setup, so there's nothing pre-programmed or hidden.

This is what real open-source engineering looks like: solving problems without sacrificing transparency.

# Audits That Anyone Can Read

Most companies hide their security audits. We don’t.

With the Keystone Pro, we published our audit reports and fixed every issue highlighted. For Keystone 3 Pro, we’re doing it again — this time with security firms [SlowMist (firmware)](https://www.slowmist.com/) and [Keylabs (hardware)](https://keylabs.io/). 

# Closed Source = Closed Doors (And Open Risks)

Recent hacks in the crypto world tell a clear story:
Closed-source software creates blind spots.

Let’s look at a few examples:

- Slope Wallet: $4.5 million lost due to private key exposure.
- MyAlgo Wallet: $9.2 million drained — even the devs didn’t know how it happened.
- Atomic Wallet: Over $35 million stolen. Cause? Still unclear.

In all three cases, the wallets were closed source. Auditors and users had no way to inspect the code before disaster struck.

Open source can’t guarantee security — but it gives the community a fighting chance to spot risks before they become headlines.

# What’s New in Keystone 3 Pro?

With the Keystone 3 Pro, we’ve made our code even easier to audit and verify.

# ✅ From Android to Embedded System

Instead of building on Android, which is bloated and hard to audit — we created a lean embedded system, **written largely in Rust, a language known for minimizing bugs.**

This makes our firmware more secure, more reproducible, and easier to inspect. Anyone can now verify that the firmware on their device matches the open-source code we publish.

# ✅ Hardware Transparency

Our commitment to transparency extends well beyond software:

**Circuit layouts**: Complete electrical schematics available for inspection
**Bill of materials:** Every component documented and verified
**Secure Element firmware:** Full access to our security chip implementation
**Application framework:** Complete development environment transparency

Our philosophy is straightforward: if we're asking you to trust us with your digital assets, you deserve complete visibility into how we protect them.
