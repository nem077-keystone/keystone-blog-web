In the world of crypto, "Not your keys, not your coins" is the golden rule. But as the industry evolves, simply holding your keys is no longer enough. We demand a more transparent, secure, and seamless interaction experience.

Today, we’re conducting a deep-dive comparison between our flagship **Keystone 3 Pro** and two prominent members of the Ledger family: **Ledger Stax** and ​**Ledger Nano X**​.


---

#### **1. Security Philosophy: Open Source vs. Closed Source**

At Keystone, we firmly believe: **Security should not be built on "Trust," but on "Verifiability."**

* **Keystone 3 Pro:** Our core code—including firmware and hardware designs—is ​**100% Open Source**​. This means developers and security experts worldwide can audit our code to ensure there are no backdoors. Furthermore, we utilize **three** Secure Element (SE) chips to safeguard your assets.
* **Ledger (Stax & Nano X):** Ledger has long maintained a closed-source operating system (BOLOS). While their SE chips are powerful, users must rely on "blind trust" in the company. This lack of transparency means users cannot independently verify the safety of their private keys. The recent "Ledger Recover" controversy further highlights the inherent risks of closed-source systems in private key management.

#### **2. User Experience: Large Touchscreen vs. Legacy Buttons**

* **Keystone 3 Pro:** Features a ​**4-inch color touchscreen**​. Using a Keystone feels as fluid as using a smartphone. More importantly, the large display allows for **Full-Text Transaction Details** (What You See Is What You Sign), effectively eliminating the risks associated with "blind signing."
* **Ledger Stax:** While the Stax introduced an E-ink touchscreen with a futuristic look, its refresh rate is noticeably low. The high cost of the E-ink display feels like "form over function"—paying a premium for aesthetics (like NFT wallpapers) rather than core security enhancements.
* **Ledger Nano X:** It retains the tiny OLED screen and dual-button navigation, which essentially feels like a relic of the past era. Handling complex DeFi transactions or multi-chain signatures on such a small screen is not only inefficient but also prone to costly manual errors.

#### **3. Signing Method: 100% Air-Gapped vs. Bluetooth/USB**

This is the fundamental architectural difference between Keystone and Ledger.

* **Keystone 3 Pro:** Employs a **100% Air-Gapped** solution. All data is transmitted via ​**QR codes**​. The device never needs to physically connect to the internet, a computer, or a phone. No Bluetooth, no USB data link—completely eliminating potential attacks via physical interfaces.
* **Ledger Nano X & Stax:** Both rely on **Bluetooth** or **USB** connections. While convenient, any physical or wireless connection theoretically opens a window for remote attacks or firmware exploits.

#### **4. Ecosystem & Multi-chain Support**

* **Keystone 3 Pro:** We support 5,000+ tokens and offer deep integration with industry leaders like ​**MetaMask (Mobile & Extension), OKX Wallet, Rabby, and BlueWallet**​. Keystone is currently the most compatible hardware wallet for the EVM and Bitcoin ecosystems (supporting Multi-sig, PSBT, etc.).
* **Ledger:** Their "Ledger Live" ecosystem is beginner-friendly. However, their closed-source nature often slows down support for new protocols (like Ordinals or specific L2s). Relying on a proprietary software-hardware stack can also raise concerns about "vendor lock-in" and centralized control.

#### **5. Advanced Features: Biometrics & Anti-Tamper**

* **Fingerprint Recognition:** Keystone 3 Pro features a built-in fingerprint module for lightning-fast unlocking and signing, including a fingerprint self-destruct mechanism. Ledger devices currently lack any biometric support.
* **Self-Destruct Mechanism:** If the Keystone casing is forcibly tampered with, the internal security chips will automatically erase all sensitive data to thwart physical attacks.
