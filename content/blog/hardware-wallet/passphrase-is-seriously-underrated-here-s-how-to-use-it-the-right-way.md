A compromised mnemonic is one of the worst things that can happen to a crypto user. But even if your seed phrase is exposed, it doesn’t have to be game over.
There’s a simple way to add an extra layer of protection, and it comes built into many hardware wallets–the Passphrase feature.

Here’s how it works, why it matters, and how to use it:

### What is a Passphrase ?

Think of your wallet like a room. Your mnemonic (seed phrase) is the key to the room. If someone has it, they can unlock the door.
A Passphrase is like an extra code added to the lock—something only you know. Even if someone gets the key, they can’t get in without that added code. It turns your wallet into multiple rooms.
Same key, but different access levels.


### Why Use a Passphrase?

The Passphrase has one job: protect your real assets.
It lets you split your holdings across multiple wallets, all derived from the same mnemonic, but each accessed with a different Passphrase.


![PASSPHRASE 1 (1).jpg](/images/blog/hardware-wallet/understand-the-passphrase-feature-of-the-keystone-hardware-wallet/passphrase-1-1-11160ea79d.jpg)


Here’s how it works:
• Mnemonic + no passphrase → Wallet A
• Mnemonic + “passphrase123” → Wallet B
• Mnemonic + “123456” → Wallet C
• Mnemonic + “abcde12345” → Wallet D

Each wallet is separate. Each requires the correct Passphrase.
If someone steals your seed phrase, they’ll likely access Wallet A—the default. You can choose to store only a small amount there, making it a decoy.
Your real funds? They stay protected in Wallets B, C, and D—hidden behind Passphrases only you know.



### Smart Strategy: Divide and Protect

Use Passphrases to organize wallets based on how much risk you can tolerate:
• Wallet A (no Passphrase) → Small amount, acts as bait or for emergencies.
• Wallet B, C, D (with Passphrases) → Main holdings, protected.
If forced to reveal a wallet, give up Wallet A. Your critical funds stay untouched.

### How To Set Up a Passphrase Wallet on Keystone 3 Pro?
• Power on your Keystone 3 Pro.
• Tap the menu icon (“...”).
• Go to Device Settings → Wallet Settings.
• Select Passphrase.

![图片.png](/images/blog/hardware-wallet/understand-the-passphrase-feature-of-the-keystone-hardware-wallet/5d798e34c3.png)

• Enter a secure Passphrase using the on-screen keyboard

![图片 (1).png](/images/blog/hardware-wallet/understand-the-passphrase-feature-of-the-keystone-hardware-wallet/1-a780cc1950.png)

• Authenticate with your fingerprint or re-enter the Passphrase.
• You’ll now have a new wallet tied to that Passphrase.

You can tell which wallet you’re in using “MFP,” a unique ID shown on the device.
>**Note:**Important: Keystone doesn’t store your Passphrase. You’ll need to enter it every time.


![图片 (2).png](/images/blog/hardware-wallet/understand-the-passphrase-feature-of-the-keystone-hardware-wallet/2-f60028b5ba.png)

### Safe Use Tips
A strong Passphrase is your last line of defense. Don’t mess it up.
• Avoid easy guesses like “123456”.
• Use a long, random string of letters/numbers.
• Back it up offline, never on your phone or cloud.
• Be case-accurate. One wrong character creates a different wallet.
• Never store your mnemonic and Passphrase in the same place.
