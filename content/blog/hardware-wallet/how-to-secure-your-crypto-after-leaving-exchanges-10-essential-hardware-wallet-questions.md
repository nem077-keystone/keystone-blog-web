**“Not your keys, not your coins.”** The moment you decide to withdraw your assets from a centralized exchange to a hardware wallet, you transition from a "holder" to a true "owner." This shift brings unparalleled security, but it also means you bear full responsibility for your wealth.

In the world of self-custody, beginners are often paralyzed by technical jargon and the fear that a single mistake could result in total loss. To eliminate this uncertainty, we’ve compiled the 10 most critical questions every newcomer asks. By the end of this guide, you’ll understand the core logic of digital asset protection and move beyond blind operation.

---

## 1. Is my crypto actually "stored" inside the hardware wallet?

**The truth: No.** This is the most common misconception.

Your assets always live on the ​**blockchain**​. The hardware wallet stores your ​**Private Keys**​—the unique digital signature required to authorize any movement of your funds.

* **The Wallet:** Your key cabinet.
* **The Private Key:** The physical key to your bank vault.
* **The Blockchain:** The vault where the assets are actually recorded.

## 2. If I lose or break my hardware wallet, is my crypto gone forever?

**Not as long as you have your "Recovery Phrase" (Seed Phrase).**

A hardware wallet is just a tool to access the blockchain. If you lose your Keystone, you simply buy a new device (or use any wallet that supports the BIP39 standard), enter your ​**24-word recovery phrase**​, and your assets will instantly be accessible again.

## 3. If the recovery phrase is all I need, why do I need a hardware wallet?

Because **online environments are never 100% secure.**

If you generate a recovery phrase on a phone or computer (a "hot wallet"), malware could log your keystrokes or record your screen. A hardware wallet provides **"Offline Generation"** and **"Offline Signing,"** ensuring your private keys never—not even for a second—touch the internet.

## 4. What does Keystone’s "Air-gapped" feature actually mean?

This is a core security pillar of Keystone.

Most hardware wallets connect via USB or Bluetooth. Keystone stays completely isolated, communicating solely through ​**QR codes**​.

* **The Benefit:** It physically blocks all potential attack paths. Even if your computer is infected with a virus, it cannot "jump" through a QR code to compromise your wallet.

## 5. What happens if Keystone (the company) goes out of business?

We adhere strictly to the ​**BIP39 industry standard**​.

The recovery phrase generated by Keystone is compatible with any other wallet following the same standard (like Ledger, Trezor, or various open-source software wallets). Your assets are not "locked" to our brand; you have absolute sovereignty over your funds.

## 6. Can I take a photo of my recovery phrase to save it on my phone?

**Absolutely not. Never take a digital copy.** This is the most frequent fatal mistake. If your cloud storage is breached or a malicious app gains access to your gallery, your assets are gone.

> **The Right Way:** Write it down on a physical card or use a stainless steel backup (like the Keystone Tablet) and store it in a secure physical location known only to you.

## 7. What is the "25th Word" (Passphrase)?

Think of it as **a custom extension to your recovery phrase.**

Even if someone steals your 24-word list, they cannot access your funds without this extra string of characters. It creates a "hidden" wallet, adding a powerful layer of protection against physical theft or coercion.

## 8. Can I trade or swap crypto directly using a hardware wallet?

Yes, but you use a software interface to initiate the process.

The hardware wallet handles the **signing** (approval), while a software app (the "watch-only" wallet) provides the interface. Keystone integrates seamlessly with ​**MetaMask, OKX Wallet, BlueWallet**​, and others. You initiate the trade on your phone, scan the QR code with Keystone to confirm, and the transaction is broadcasted securely.

## 9. Are firmware updates safe? Could they contain malware?

Keystone uses a **multi-verification mechanism.**

Updates are performed via a MicroSD card, and every official firmware release is protected by a unique digital signature. The device hardware automatically verifies this signature; if the firmware has been tampered with, the device will refuse to boot.

## 10. What is the first thing I should do after getting my device?

**Perform a "Recovery Test."**

1. Follow the instructions to generate your recovery phrase.
2. ​**Before transferring large amounts**​, send a tiny test amount (e.g., \$5).
3. Factory reset your device.
4. Recover the wallet using your written 24 words.
5. If your test balance appears, you have confirmed your backup is 100% accurate.
